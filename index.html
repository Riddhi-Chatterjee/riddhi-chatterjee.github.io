About

I am a software engineer at RingCentral in Bangalore. In my own time, alongside full-time work, I do research with Prof. Viswanath Gopalakrishnan at IIIT Bangalore on goal images for robot manipulation.

In our project, SGE-Goal, a model generates the goal image, the picture that shows a robot policy what the end of a task should look like. It first predicts where the object starts and where it has to end up, and only then draws the image. When we compared nine goal generators on CALVIN, the goals that helped the policy most were the ones that moved the right object to the right place, not the most realistic ones.

That result left me with questions I want to work on next. Can a goal generator be trained for the success of the policy that carries out its goals, and should the generator and the policy adapt to each other over long tasks? If task success drives the training, do the goals stay faithful pictures of the intended end state, or drift into codes only one policy reads?

I graduated from IIIT Bangalore’s integrated M.Tech programme in Computer Science and Engineering in 2025, with the Sitaram Jindal Gold Medal as the best outgoing integrated M.Tech. student, awarded for CGPA. I am looking for a PhD position starting in autumn 2027.

Research

Goal Images Are Control Targets: Spatially-Grounded Goal-Image Synthesis for Robotic Manipulation

Riddhi Chatterjee, Ritish Shrirao, Kushagra Mehrotra, Viswanath Gopalakrishnan

WACV 2027, accepted in the first round, to appear. First author.

SGE-Goal predicts an initial and a final edit-region mask, then renders the goal image conditioned on both, so that spatial decisions come before appearance. On CALVIN, with small open models, it comes within half a point of the ground-truth goal on single steps (73.1% against 73.6%) and reaches about 7× the success rate of the best proprietary editor, Gemini 3 Pro Image, at five chained steps.
